# Participatory governance platforms: decisions for Engage

Research date: 13 November 2026. Status: research only; no feature implementation or governance change authorised by this document.

Read alongside the [external evidence register](participatory-platforms-evidence.md), which records platform-specific observations, sources, benefits, costs and limitations.

## Decision summary

Engage already implements much of the important governance separation. Keep its distinction between policy proposals, governance resolutions, deliberative stances, formal amendments, recorded ballot outcomes and ratified policy. The strongest opportunities concern evidence and comprehension: make an incorporation claim verifiable, distinguish historical access from active discovery, and define what privacy aggregate reporting actually promises.

Do not import a municipal participation suite or replace Engage's governance rules with another platform's defaults. Decidim provides useful accountability relationships; CONSUL illustrates separate retirement and archive concepts; Loomio shows how a conversation, poll and announced outcome can be connected without being treated as the same thing. None establishes authority under Engage's constitution.

## Method and evidence limits

- External evidence uses official manuals and inspected public source files. See the register for individual links. Documentation describes supported behaviour; source inspection establishes implementation paths, not successful execution in a deployed instance. Moving branch URLs and development manuals are not immutable release evidence.
- Engage was inspected locally, initially at `a9551202cbec7450ab102919409fafe2bacc4666`, then at `0b36edf358f264d6c39cfe568e18c84429162e4d`. Another work stream committed contribution-response notifications during this investigation. Those notifications are included in the comparison, not proposed as missing work.
- REA tool discovery found no exposed REA MCP tools. The attempted browser visit to `https://meta.decidim.org/` failed because the browser access host was unavailable. The web text fetch also returned 403 for that site. No runtime interception, instrumentation, authenticated administration, ballot submission or actual interactive journey was verified.
- Public page text was inspected at [Decide Madrid](https://decide.madrid.es/), its linked [help centre](https://decide.madrid.es/mas-informacion), and [Loomio Community](https://www.loomio.com/community). Madrid exposes participation areas and a help route; Loomio exposes a public discussion list and a link to its collaboratively decided moderation policy. These are content observations, not visual or usability test results. Madrid's fetched page contained loading placeholders; the policy discussion link could not be fetched. No claim is made about successful form completion, mobile layout, accessibility conformance or console/network health.
- No production data, membership-size statistics or operational response-time data were inspected. Scale judgements below assume a member organisation with existing branch administration, rather than a city-wide participatory budgeting programme. Revisit them if operational evidence changes.
- No proprietary code was copied. The reports describe patterns independently; they do not propose importing upstream implementations.

## Findings compared against Engage

### 1. Separate the subject, the decision and the authority

**Observed externally:** Decidim separates proposals/amendments from linked accountability results; CONSUL separates citizen proposals, collaborative legislation and polls; Loomio separates discussions, polls and outcomes. The evidence register explains the different meanings of “proposal” and “vote” in each product.

**Advantage:** Members can understand whether they are suggesting wording, expressing support, voting or viewing an adopted decision. **Disadvantage:** More objects and statuses can make navigation and authority harder to understand.

**Engage relevance:** Keep its existing types, immutable wording and ratification boundaries. A governance resolution need not become a new municipal-style participation module. Consider a concise linked decision summary showing wording voted on, recorded outcome, deciding authority, ratification and successor when relevant. This would improve presentation of existing records rather than collapse them into one “accepted” state. Acceptance criteria should verify that a successful ballot is never labelled adopted policy before ratification, and a comment stance never counts as a ballot.

### 2. Close the response loop with evidence, not another status taxonomy

**Observed externally:** Decidim has official proposal answers and traceable amendments/results; CONSUL has different mechanisms for proposal notifications and budget-project assessment/progress; Loomio allows a facilitator to announce a poll outcome. These mechanisms address different stages of participation and should not be treated as equivalent official decisions.

**Advantage:** People can see an accountable response and what happened next. **Disadvantage:** An answer or outcome can overstate authority or incorporation unless it links to the underlying change and names who made the claim.

**Engage relevance:** It already has a richer comment-response vocabulary than a simple accepted/rejected answer. Add optional structured evidence to an incorporated response: a visible revision comparison, applied amendment or policy reference. Use an existing permission-checked record picker rather than requiring an internal ID. Keep the response's actor/time; do not imply a proposal author's acknowledgement is constitutional approval. An amendment already records `appliedRevisionId` in [`apply.ts`](../../src/pages/api/admin/amendments/apply.ts), so reuse that evidence rather than duplicate it.

The current note is optional and can merely assert incorporation. A future design should allow a short explanation for declined/deferred responses and evidence for incorporation without forcing authors through a long form. Verify that inaccessible linked records reveal no title or content, and clearing a response preserves its history. Notification delivery and catch-up already exist; assess their usefulness before expanding cadence.

### 3. Keep history without keeping everything in active lists

**Observed externally:** Decidim retains withdrawn proposals as a distinguishable state; CONSUL distinguishes explained retirement from date-based archiving; Loomio distinguishes a closed conversation/poll from deletion. None of these facts proves complete, immutable legal records or a general supersession graph across all three products.

**Advantage:** Old decisions remain intelligible while active participation stays focused. **Disadvantage:** Broad historical access can expose formerly restricted content or make withdrawn wording look authoritative.

**Engage relevance:** The real question is access policy. Ordinary members currently cannot read archived proposal detail or revisions, and withdrawn amendments are filtered from the normal register even though their records persist. Published-policy history has its own status-aware page, but retired approved wording is hidden there. These are observed choices, not evidence of data deletion.

Before implementation, agree who should see historical wording, withdrawal reasons and predecessor records. A possible design is an authorised read-only history view, clear “no longer current” status, and a successor link, with active discovery continuing to omit retired items. Do not simply enable `includeArchived` for everyone. Any change to access, withdrawal authority or formal decision semantics needs an explicit governance specification. Review successor chains and cycle handling separately; inspection found self-link and existence checks in the quick update path, not a demonstrated whole-chain invariant.

### 4. Prioritise the next useful action over catalogue breadth

**Observed externally:** The inspected Madrid page groups participation by activity; Loomio Community exposes discussion topics and a link to a real prior collective decision. Decidim's manuals describe process/component navigation and proposal creation support. Actual first-time completion rates were not measured.

**Advantage:** Task/context grouping can help someone enter participation. **Disadvantage:** A municipal catalogue, multiple modules and many navigation choices can be excessive for Engage.

**Engage relevance:** Preserve “Needs my vote”, actionable proposal heroes, branch context, paging and concise empty states. Conduct a small moderated study before redesign: ask a new member to find one eligible ballot, distinguish a comment from an amendment, find a response to their contribution, and locate the current successor policy. Include a non-eligible account, keyboard use and mobile. Measure task success and misunderstandings, not just clicks. Treat additional filters, tours and recommendation algorithms as hypotheses until this reveals a problem.

### 5. State the privacy model before expanding reporting

**Observed externally:** Decidim's privacy commitments do not make ordinary proposal supports equivalent to secret elections. CONSUL's inspected poll-answer model associates answers with an author, so a statistical report alone cannot establish anonymous storage. Loomio's anonymous-poll design provides a materially different identity/choice separation and constrains functionality; see the register's source-specific limits.

**Advantage:** Separating aggregate operational reporting from ballot identities reduces routine exposure. **Disadvantage:** Even aggregates can reveal a choice through small electorates, unanimous results or before/after comparisons. Stronger anonymity changes audit, amendment, voting-change and recovery capabilities.

**Engage relevance:** Its current role restriction is appropriate to preserve pending an authorised alternative. Do not call the ballots anonymous. Review report fields, time windows, exports and participation feeds together, including how the denominator is defined: a branch's verified membership is not automatically the eligible electorate for every ballot. Small-group suppression or delayed totals may be useful, but choose thresholds against a documented threat model rather than copy Loomio's number. Include differencing across overlapping filters and successive exports; a single minimum-cell-size rule is insufficient.

## Prioritised recommendations

Priorities are recommendations for further design, not implementation approval. Effort is relative and excludes governance consultation.

| Priority / category | Recommendation | Why now; cost or disadvantage | Required evidence before shipping |
|---|---|---|---|
| Keep: already strong | Preserve distinct policy/resolution, discussion/ballot, amendment/wording revision and ballot-result/ratification/publication boundaries. | Existing code already meets the core separation need. Renaming or collapsing states could introduce governance errors. | Existing permission, revision-binding and governance regressions remain green; member comprehension check. |
| Keep: already strong | Retain contribution responses/history/notifications, supersession metadata, policy publication events, eligibility-aware discovery and aggregate CSV. | These are existing capabilities, not a competitive feature backlog. | Verify full rendered workflows when browser access is restored; current source/test evidence does not prove polished UX. |
| P1: genuine opportunity | Link “incorporated” feedback to a concrete wording change or decision record. | High trust value, small-to-medium change reusing existing records; extra selection effort for responders. | Member can follow contribution → changed wording; unauthorised evidence stays private; author acknowledgement is clearly distinct from official adoption. |
| P1: genuine opportunity, governance decision first | Specify historical access and withdrawal presentation across proposals, amendments and policy records. | Current retention/access differs by object. Medium scope; greater visibility may expose sensitive deliberation. | Agreed access matrix, withdrawn-amendment example, archived proposal example, superseded-policy example; no new permission inferred from navigation. |
| P1: genuine opportunity, policy definition first | Document confidentiality and review small-group inference before adding finer reporting. | Affects member trust; existing aggregate export and administrator-only identities are a good baseline. Limits may reduce useful reporting. | Named roles and threat model, cohort/differencing tests, accurate privacy copy; no change to identifiable-ballot boundary without specification. |
| P2: validate opportunity | Bring existing decision/ratification/successor evidence into a concise decision summary. | Reduces member interpretation effort; risks another crowded panel if shown indiscriminately. | A member can identify what passed and what is current; no duplicate lifecycle system or raw event IDs in ordinary UI. |
| P2: validate opportunity | Test first-time journeys before changing discovery. | Existing next-action and voting focus are promising. Research costs time but can prevent unnecessary redesign. | Observed task success, permission-sensitive states, mobile/keyboard coverage and failed-action recovery. |
| P2: conditional opportunity | Add minimal implementation progress only if adopted commitments routinely need follow-up. | Decidim's linked results offer a useful model; a new owner/date/progress workflow creates administrative work. | Evidence of stale commitments or repeated member questions; trial a short note plus evidence before milestones/percentages. |
| Defer: unnecessary at assumed scale | Municipal participatory budgeting, territorial maps, multiple participation-space builders, proposal-signature thresholds and broad poll-method catalogues. | No demonstrated Engage requirement; substantial complexity and potential conflict with constitutional rules. | Reconsider only with an actual programme, owner, budget and governance specification. |
| Defer: unnecessary at assumed scale | Gamified leaderboards, complex recommendation ranking and detailed participation profiling. | Existing filters and catch-up address discovery; rankings can privilege prolific contributors and increase privacy exposure. | A measured discovery problem and an equitable, privacy-reviewed alternative. |
| Longer-term architecture | Explicit provenance relationships between contributions, amendments, revisions, decisions and published policy. | Useful if many contributions feed several decisions; avoid a generic graph framework until real relationships require it. | Stable domain terms and concrete cross-record queries; begin with simple typed references. |
| Longer-term architecture | Reconciled, append-oriented governance events and rebuildable reporting projections. | Existing revisions and publication events provide a start. Could improve audit and operational recovery; event sourcing adds complexity. | Demonstrated recovery/concurrency/reporting need; transactional consistency analysis and replay rules before migration. |
| Longer-term architecture, explicit governance mandate | Separate eligibility/participation records from ballot choices for a genuinely anonymous ballot mode. | Loomio demonstrates meaningful constraints; Engage presently permits authorised identification and vote updates. This is a new privacy/governance model, not a UI toggle. | Approved secrecy, verifiability, audit, recovery and revoting requirements; independent security review. |

## Verification and unanswered questions

Targeted existing tests passed: 15/15 across proposal discovery, contributor feedback/supersession, proposal access/ballot privacy and admin reporting. These use development storage and test doubles; they do not establish deployed MongoDB behaviour or third-party runtime behaviour. Whole-project tests also passed: 186 passed, 15 skipped, 0 failed (201 total). `npm run check` reported 0 errors, 0 warnings and 33 hints. Skipped integrations mean live MongoDB and external-provider guarantees remain unverified. No lint script is defined in `package.json`.

All five research questions have documentation/source evidence and an Engage comparison. Runtime and rendered-journey coverage remain partial. Outstanding questions are: which historical wording members should be entitled to inspect; whether policy withdrawal suppresses historical text intentionally; how often contribution claims lack evidence; actual organisational/reporting scale; and whether members expect confidentiality from peers or secrecy even from administrators. Resolve these through governance decisions and observation before implementing the recommendations.
